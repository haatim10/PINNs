\begin{abstract}
This paper studies memory-aware physics-informed neural solvers for a time-fractional integro-differential equation with weakly singular history terms. We compare a classical PINN baseline, TE-QPINN-inspired surrogate variants, and analytic memory-feature extensions under a fairness-controlled protocol with matched seeds, collocation points, and training budgets. Across Adam-only 10-seed confirmatory evaluation, the strongest mean accuracy is achieved by the classical model with analytic memory features (final L2 $0.752910\pm0.188977$), while memory-aware TE variants improve over non-memory TE baselines but do not surpass the classical+memory control. Paired testing supports the classical-memory improvement over the classical baseline (Wilcoxon two-sided $p=0.0371$). A robustness slice at $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.7,0.3)$ indicates that memory-feature benefits transfer more clearly on the classical track than on the TE track. We also include an exact PennyLane TE-QPINN feasibility study: forward and higher-order derivative paths are differentiable, but naive simulator-based execution is currently too slow for practical large-scale benchmarking. Finally, synthetic sparse CSI forecasting case studies (scalar and two-channel) show that memory/domain-informed features improve application-inspired prediction quality. No claim of quantum advantage is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Time-fractional integro-differential systems are challenging for neural solvers because their operators are nonlocal in time and encode long-range memory effects. In practice, this means that approximation quality depends not only on local derivatives but also on stable handling of fractional-history terms and weakly singular kernels. These coupled difficulties make such systems a useful stress test for physics-informed learning methods.

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) are a natural starting point because they enforce governing equations through residual-based objectives and can be trained without full mesh-based solvers \cite{PINNs,FracPINN}. However, baseline PINNs may still struggle when memory effects are strong or when representation bias is mismatched to the operator structure. This motivates two complementary extensions: architecture-level changes inspired by TE-QPINN \cite{QNN,QPINN,TEQPINN}, and feature-level priors that encode fractional-memory structure directly.

This final report is built on a staged development history from the earlier project phase. The earlier stage established a reliable fractional-PINN workflow (manufactured solution, IC/BC/PDE loss structure, and baseline convergence checks), including a simpler midpoint-style treatment of the Volterra memory integral and exploratory architecture choices. The present work keeps that mathematical foundation fixed, then upgrades the nonlocal residual treatment with kernel-aware product integration and evaluates TE-inspired and memory-aware extensions under stricter multi-seed fairness and statistical validation.

Accordingly, this work builds from a classical fractional PINN and evaluates three questions: (i) whether TE-QPINN-inspired surrogate components improve performance under controlled budgets, (ii) whether analytic memory-aware features provide robust gains, and (iii) how much of any gain is attributable to feature design versus TE-specific architecture. To answer these questions, we include a classical+memory fairness control, staged ablations, multi-seed confirmatory tests, and paired statistical analysis.

Methodologically, we retain the same fractional PDE backbone as the earlier project phase: Caputo/L1 discretization context, product-integration treatment of weakly singular Volterra terms, and manufactured-solution consistency \cite{FracCalc,L1Scheme,ProductIntegration}. We further add an exact PennyLane TE-QPINN feasibility check and a synthetic sparse-CSI forecasting extension to probe application-oriented transfer \cite{CSI,MIMO,LEO}.

The mid-semester baseline work provides the design template for this final report: start from a mathematically transparent fractional PINN, then progressively improve the nonlocal residual discretization (especially the Volterra memory term), and finally evaluate architecture-level extensions under strict fairness controls rather than unconstrained hyperparameter tuning.

\section{Fractional Integro-Differential Problem Formulation}
We consider the baseline problem
\begin{equation}
    D_t^\alpha u(x,t) - (x^2+1)u_{xx}(x,t) +
    \int_0^t \sin(x)(t-s)^{-\beta}u(x,s)\,ds = f(x,t),
\end{equation}
with $x\in[0,1],\, t\in(0,1]$, boundary conditions
\begin{equation}
u(0,t)=t^\alpha,\quad u(1,t)=-t^\alpha,
\end{equation}
and initial condition
\begin{equation}
u(x,0)=0.
\end{equation}
Here $D_t^\alpha(\cdot)$ is the Caputo derivative ($0<\alpha<1$), written as
\begin{equation}
D_t^\alpha u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_0^t (t-s)^{-\alpha}\,\partial_s u(x,s)\,ds,
\end{equation}
which explicitly encodes nonlocal-in-time memory behavior.
The Volterra kernel term $(t-s)^{-\beta}$ is weakly singular but integrable for $0<\beta<1$, which is the main reason naive quadrature can become unstable near the upper integration limit even when the PDE is well posed.

The exact-solution family used for manufactured forcing in the benchmark code is
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{exact}}(x,t)=t^\alpha\cos(\pi x),
\end{equation}
which is the primary manufactured solution used in the core benchmark. For implementation support and controlled difficulty sweeps, the code also supports the generalized family
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{exact}}(x,t)=A\,t^{p}\,\cos(k\pi x+\phi),
\end{equation}
with default branch settings $A=1$, $p=\alpha$, $k=1$, $\phi=0$, and optional variants for harder oscillatory behavior. The forcing $f(x,t)$ is generated analytically to match this family under the full operator, so IC/BC/PDE residual evaluations remain self-consistent across model variants.

\section{Baseline PINN and Numerical Residual Construction}
The baseline PINN maps $(x,t)\mapsto \hat u(x,t)$ and minimizes a composite objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\lambda_{\text{pde}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pde}}+
\lambda_{\text{bc}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{bc}}+
\lambda_{\text{ic}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ic}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pde}}$ is built from the residual of the full fractional integro-differential operator.

Training uses three point sets: interior collocation points for PDE residual, boundary points for $u(0,t),u(1,t)$, and initial points for $u(x,0)$. The residual term is evaluated at collocation points and combined with BC/IC penalties under fixed loss weights for fair cross-model comparison.

For the Caputo component, the implementation uses an L1-style history approximation on a graded temporal mesh (to better resolve near-origin singular behavior) \cite{L1Scheme,FracCalc}. The graded mesh is of the form $t_n=T(n/N)^r$ with grading exponent $r\ge 1$, which increases resolution near $t=0$ where fractional kernels are most singular.

For the weakly singular Volterra term, the integral is evaluated with kernel-aware product integration: on each interval $[t_k,t_{k+1}]$, $u(x,s)$ is linearly interpolated while kernel moments are integrated analytically. This yields a discrete memory operator of the form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_n[u](x)\approx\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} w_{n,k}\,\hat u(x,t_k,t_{k+1}),
\end{equation}
where $w_{n,k}$ are kernel-dependent weights and $\hat u(x,t_k,t_{k+1})$ denotes the local linear reconstruction. This reduces singular-kernel quadrature error while preserving differentiability with respect to network parameters \cite{ProductIntegration}.

This design is intentionally grounded in the mid-semester progression: an earlier midpoint-style approximation was useful for initial solver validation, but kernel-aware product integration became necessary once larger grids and stronger memory effects were targeted.

\section{TE-QPINN-Inspired and Exact PQC Model Variants}
\subsection{TE-QPINN-Inspired Surrogate Family}
The TE-inspired surrogate is a classical PyTorch model that emulates TE-QPINN design ideas without using a true quantum circuit. Its core pipeline is: (i) input rescaling, (ii) trainable embedding network, (iii) angle-style encoding, (iv) sinusoidal and pairwise mixing features, and (v) expectation-style readout with optional residual blending.

Inputs are affine-rescaled to a stable range (default approximately $[-0.95,0.95]$). A trainable embedding MLP outputs $\phi_i(x,t)$ and constructs angle-style coordinates
\begin{equation}
\theta_i=\phi_i(x,t)\cdot \tilde z_i,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde z_i$ cycles over scaled coordinates. Quantum-inspired features include $\sin(\theta_i)$, $\cos(\theta_i)$, and entanglement-inspired pairwise terms such as $\sin(\theta_i)\cos(\theta_{i+1})$. A variational mixing block and expectation-style readout head then produce the final prediction.

Residual correction is configurable: fixed additive residual branch or learned gated residual blending. Additional stabilization includes optional LayerNorm at post-quantum or post-entanglement positions. These switches enable controlled ablation without changing the PDE residual pipeline or fairness protocol.

\subsection{Exact PennyLane TE-QPINN Variant}
An optional exact simulator-based variant is implemented with PennyLane (default.qubit backend), using trainable embedding angles, per-wire rotation blocks (RX/RY/RZ), CNOT entanglement, and expectation-value readout \cite{QNN,QPINN,TEQPINN}. This model is treated as a feasibility track rather than the main benchmark track. In this repository, exact-PQC correctness was verified through forward pass checks, gradient flow checks, and higher-order derivative checks ($u_x$ and $u_{xx}$), then evaluated in a tiny-smoke comparison to quantify practical runtime limits.
This exact variant is simulator-based and separate from the surrogate path; no hardware-quantum or quantum-speedup claim is made.

\section{Memory-Aware Analytic Feature Design}
The memory-aware feature set augments local coordinates with fractional-structure-inspired terms:
\begin{equation}
\phi_{\text{mem}}(x,t)=\left[x,t,t^\alpha,t^{1-\alpha},t^{1-\beta},\log(1+t),xt,xt^\alpha\right].
\end{equation}
Near $t=0$, safety clipping uses $t_{\text{safe}}=\max(t,\epsilon)$ (default $\epsilon=10^{-8}$). Feature normalization is deterministic (range-based scaling) rather than batch-adaptive, so train/eval behavior remains consistent. The same feature builder is shared across TE and classical-memory control runs to avoid implementation asymmetry.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Fairness Controls}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Identical problem setup and residual construction across compared variants.
    \item Locked Adam-only budget for primary fairness comparisons (12 epochs on 50$\times$50 setting).
    \item Separate labeling for extended-budget optimizer studies (Adam+LBFGS); these are not equal-runtime claims.
    \item Same seed lists, same train/test points per seed, and explicit parameter/runtime reporting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Tracks}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Core benchmark: 50$\times$50 fractional PDE setup.
    \item Multi-seed protocol: 5-seed screening and 10-seed confirmatory validation.
    \item Robustness slice at $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.7,0.3)$.
    \item Seed-42 ablations for stabilization diagnostics.
    \item Optional exact-PQC tiny-smoke feasibility check.
    \item Application-inspired sparse CSI demos (scalar and two-channel).
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
The benchmark evidence indicates that analytic memory features are the most reliable improvement mechanism in this study. On the core 10-seed confirmatory benchmark, classical+memory is strongest by mean L2/Linf and remains far more efficient than TE variants. TE memory-aware models improve over non-memory TE baselines in the base setting, but they do not establish superiority over classical+memory under fairness-controlled Adam-only conditions.
The classical+memory control is therefore essential: it isolates memory-feature contribution from TE-architecture contribution and prevents attributing feature-engineering gains to TE design alone.

Stabilization experiments show that TE-side performance is sensitive to architectural details. LayerNorm provides consistent TE improvement relative to fixed TE, while gated blending and residual-scale changes that looked promising in single-seed diagnostics do not generalize robustly across locked multi-seed comparisons. This reinforces the need for strict protocol separation between exploratory diagnostics and confirmatory claims.

The exact-PQC track confirms technical feasibility but highlights a practical bottleneck: differentiability is achieved, yet runtime scales poorly in naive simulator usage for higher-order physics residuals. In the application extension, domain-informed and combined features improve synthetic sparse CSI forecasting quality; however, these results remain application-inspired rather than deployment-level communication claims.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
This work does not claim quantum advantage, universal TE superiority, or practical large-scale viability of the current exact-PQC path. The application track is synthetic and does not represent a standards-compliant LEO/MIMO simulator or real-world deployment benchmark. Statistical conclusions are strongest for the classical+memory improvement and weaker for some TE-vs-TE comparisons due to effect-size variability and sample count.

Future work should prioritize: (i) broader robustness slices over additional $(\alpha,\beta)$ regimes; (ii) equal-runtime frontier analysis in addition to equal-epoch budgets; (iii) improved exact-PQC batching/differentiation strategies; and (iv) richer application datasets and channel models while preserving fairness controls.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a fairness-controlled study of classical, TE-inspired, and memory-aware PINNs for a time-fractional integro-differential benchmark, supplemented by statistical testing, robustness checks, exact-PQC feasibility analysis, and sparse CSI application demos. The strongest and most reproducible gains come from analytic memory features, especially on the classical baseline. TE memory-aware models show meaningful progress over non-memory TE variants in the base setting but do not establish superiority over classical+memory. Exact PennyLane TE-QPINN is feasible and differentiable, yet currently computationally impractical in naive tiny-smoke execution. Overall, the results support memory-aware feature design as the most impactful near-term direction, with TE and exact-PQC tracks requiring further optimization for competitive practical performance.
